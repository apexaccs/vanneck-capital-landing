SAFE, SAFT, token warrant or hybrid: which fits your project

Founders often ask which instrument we prefer. The better question is which one fits your plan. We use four: SAFE, SAFT, token warrant and hybrid. Here is what each does in plain terms.

SAFE

A simple agreement for future equity. The investor pays now and receives shares in a later priced round, usually on set terms such as a valuation cap or discount. It fits a project whose value is in the company and that does not plan to issue a token, or has not decided.

SAFT

A simple agreement for future tokens. The investor pays now and receives tokens when the project launches them, on agreed terms. It fits a project where the token is the product and equity is secondary or absent.

Token warrant

The right to buy tokens at a set price in the future. It is often paired with an equity investment, so the investor holds equity today and an option on the token later. It fits a company that has an equity business and keeps the token as a possible later step.

Hybrid

Equity and token rights together in one package. It fits a project where both the company and the token carry value, and where the founders want one document that covers both.

How to choose

  1. If you will not launch a token, equity paper is enough.
  2. If the token is the product, a SAFT matches the plan better than equity.
  3. If you may launch a token later but are not sure, a token warrant leaves the decision open without forcing it today.
  4. If both the company and the token will matter, a hybrid avoids two separate negotiations.

Whichever you pick, read the same four terms: valuation or cap, the discount or price, the vesting and unlock schedule for the tokens, and what happens on a change of control. These decide the outcome more than the name of the instrument.
