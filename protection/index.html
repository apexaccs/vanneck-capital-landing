Consumer AV misses real threats — documented cases

Windows Defender

Nodersok used fileless techniques to infect thousands across the US and Europe without Defender raising an alert (2019). Conti ransomware executed from a Defender exclusion folder with no warning until Microsoft patched the flaw in February 2022. A SmartScreen shortcut-nesting bug spread ARC Stealer, Lumma and Meduza throughout 2024. Black Hat researchers demonstrated that intercepted signature updates could make Defender silently ignore Conti and Mimikatz.

Kaspersky

Kaspersky ranked among the top five products by missed detections in a Malwarebytes study, alongside Avast, ESET, AVG and Norton. User forum reports document samples that reached the endpoint undetected and were added to the database only after the fact.

Malwarebytes

A known malware sample detected by 44 of 55 VirusTotal engines went unnoticed by Malwarebytes (BleepingComputer, 2017). Fake MSI Afterburner installer components passed through both Defender and Malwarebytes without triggering a single detection.

Mac has no real consumer EDR option

Consumer antivirus on macOS watches what files look like. EDR watches what processes actually do in real time — memory writes, kernel calls, lateral movement. That gap is exactly where modern macOS malware operates.

Sophos brings full EDR to macOS, the same grade used by enterprise security teams, without requiring any in-house expertise to manage.

Ledger

A cold wallet can’t protect you if your device is already compromised

Attackers with device access don’t target the hardware — they replace the software. The result is indistinguishable from the real application.

Windows — April 2020

Ledger warned of malware that replaced Ledger Live locally on disk. After a fake update prompt, it requested the user’s 24-word seed phrase. There was no visible difference from a legitimate update flow.

macOS — 2024–2025

Moonlock tracked four simultaneous active campaigns. Malware families Odyssey and AMOS (Atomic macOS Stealer) replaced the genuine Ledger Live with a clone that showed a “suspicious activity” alert and prompted for the seed phrase.

Chrome extension

A counterfeit Ledger browser extension offered “wallet sync” — which required entering the full seed phrase. The difference between the genuine extension and the fake was not visible to the average user.

Network injections reach any device that has ever been online

Using a separate device for crypto does not eliminate risk if that device has ever connected to a network.

Network-layer attacks operate at the router or ISP level, intercepting traffic and injecting malicious code into unencrypted sessions. A device that has browsed the web even once can be targeted this way. Nothing changes visibly on the device — until the payload executes.

The goal isn’t to plan for what happens when malware reaches your device.
The goal is to stop it from ever getting there.
